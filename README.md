# School Performance Dashboard

## Project Overview

This dashboard was created to analyse academic performance across multiple schools using a detailed dataset of student marks by subject, topic, teacher, and term.

The goal is to move beyond simple ranking tables and provide clear, actionable insight into:

- How schools compare overall
- How individual students are performing and progressing
- How teachers influence results within subjects

The dashboard is split into three focused pages so that each audience (school leadership, academic heads, and teachers) can quickly find the information that matters most to them.

---

## Dashboard Pages

### 1. Overall School Performance (Overview Page)

**Purpose**  
To give leadership a high-level view of how schools compare on key academic indicators and to surface patterns that deserve deeper investigation.

**Key metrics explored**
- School average mark
- Pass rate
- Performance by subject
- Performance by grade
- Consistency of rankings across different measures

**Why this page exists**  
Decision-makers need a reliable starting point. This page answers the first set of business questions:  
*Which schools are performing well? Where are the gaps? Are the results consistent or driven by a single grade or subject?*

### 2. Student Performance Page

**Purpose**  
To shift the focus from the school level to the individual learner. This page supports identification of high performers, at-risk students, and progress (or decline) across terms.

**Why this page exists**  
School averages can hide important variation. Understanding student-level patterns helps target interventions, celebrate excellence, and track whether improvement is broad-based or concentrated in a few learners.

### 3. Teacher Performance Page

**Purpose**  
To examine results through the lens of the teachers delivering the subjects. This page supports professional development conversations and highlights where teaching strength or support needs exist.

**Why this page exists**  
Teachers are a critical lever in student outcomes. By linking marks to the teachers responsible for different groups, the dashboard enables more meaningful conversations about practice, consistency, and subject-specific support.

## Key Findings – Overall School Performance Page

### High-Level Rankings

The two private schools rank in the top two positions for both **pass rate** and **school average mark**.

> **Data note:** The number of students per school in this dataset is relatively small and not fully realistic for real-world school sizes. However, the dataset is still substantial because of the number of subjects, topics within each subject, and the marks recorded across three terms. Rankings should therefore be interpreted with this limitation in mind.

Rankings remain **consistent** across:
- Overall pass rate
- School average mark
- Individual subject performance per school

### Performance by Grade

When results are examined by grade, the picture becomes more nuanced:

- **Summit College Prep** remains the highest-performing school across all three grades (when using rounded grade-level comparisons).
- **Lakeside Public School** remains the lowest-performing school across all grades.
- Positions 2, 3, and 4 shift depending on the grade being examined, but 1st and 5th places stay stable.

### Deep Dive – Summit College Prep

Summit College Prep shows strong, broad-based performance rather than results driven by a single exceptional subject:

- Average mark in every subject is above 82%.
- Five out of the six subjects average 90% or higher.
- Performance is consistently high across Grades 10, 11, and 12.

This suggests academic strength that is spread across the curriculum rather than concentrated in one area.

### Deep Dive – Lakeside Public School

Lakeside is the lowest-performing school when results are viewed by grade.

Subject-level averages at Lakeside:
- Highest average subject mark: **63%** (English Language)
- Lowest average subject mark: **57%** (Life Sciences)

The lowest recorded grade-level averages (54%) appear in both Grade 11 and Grade 12.

These figures align with the overall school ranking and highlight particular pressure points in the senior grades and in Life Sciences.

---

## Data Notes & Limitations

- Student counts per school are smaller than would be expected in a real multi-school system. This affects how representative the rankings are of actual school populations.
- The large number of records comes from the combination of subjects × topics × terms rather than from a large student body.
- All students take the same six subjects across all three terms, which keeps comparisons clean but does not reflect subject choice or streaming that exists in many real schools.
